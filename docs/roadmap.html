01

Where things stand

Eighteen static pages, built from the business profile and the four certificates. No content is placeholder text — every product line, client name, office address and certificate number on the site came out of the source documents.

The architecture is deliberately boring, because a trade site earns nothing from being clever. Content lives in Markdown and JSON files; adding a product category is one file, and it propagates automatically to the products grid, the footer, the sourcing origin index, the enquiry form dropdown and the sitemap. There is no database, no server and no monthly platform bill.

Homepage weight
12 KBGzipped. Whole-site CSS is 46 KB, and no JavaScript framework ships to the browser.
Catalogue depth
10 / ~180Categories and individual product lines, each with storage regime and sourcing origins.
Proof published
4 certificatesISO 9001, ISO 22000, HACCP and DCCI — numbers, dates, scope and downloadable PDFs.

What is deliberately missing

Three things were left as slots rather than invented, because faking them would have cost credibility with exactly the buyers this site is for:

  • Photography. Categories render generated brand patterns instead of stock imagery. Drop a real file into public/images/products/ and add one image: line to swap it in.
  • Client and brand logos. Currently clean typographic tiles. They need written permission before they become real marks.
  • Prices. Nowhere on the site, and that is correct for this business — pricing is a conversation, and published prices would only help competitors.
02

Before launch

Six open items. None is large; two of them are things only World Club can answer, and the first is genuinely urgent.

The HACCP certificate contradicts itself — confirm with QBM first

The printed HACCP certificate shows 1st Surveillance audit Date: 30-07-2027 alongside Certificate Expiry Date: 26-07-2026. Those two labels appear to be transposed: the ISO 9001 and ISO 22000 certificates, issued the same day by the same body, both read expiry 30-07-2027 with surveillance audits on 26-07-2025 and 26-07-2026.

Read literally, the site would announce that World Club's HACCP certification expired last month. The site currently follows the ISO pattern instead, with the discrepancy commented in src/content/certifications/haccp.md. Get it confirmed in writing from QBM before launch — and while on the call, ask for the continuation letter for the 26 July 2026 surveillance audit, which fell due three weeks ago.

Origins were written only from countries the business profile actually names. They are reasonable but unconfirmed, and they now feed the sourcing page's origin index — so a wrong entry is visible in two places.

The domain decision is worth ten minutes of thought, not five

The profile lists arisa-worldclub.com, but ARISA is one of three own brands sitting inside World Club — alongside Fatima and Madina. Naming the parent company's website after one of its own labels will read oddly to a foreign supplier evaluating World Club as a distribution partner, and it constrains the group structure described in section 06.

A neutral company domain — worldclub.com.bd or similar — ages better. Whichever way it goes, decide before launch: changing domains afterwards means redirects, lost search history and reprinted stationery.

03

The four phases

These are sequenced deliberately: each phase depends on the one before it. Building search content before the site has photography, or a CMS before anyone needs to edit, wastes money in a predictable way.

Phase 0 Now →
2 weeks In progress

Get it live

Clear the six items in section 02 and ship. The site is more useful live and imperfect than perfect and unpublished — search engines need time to index, and that clock only starts at launch.

  • Deploy to Cloudflare Pages or Netlify. Free tier, automatic SSL, global CDN. Build command npm run build, output dist.
  • Google Search Console + Bing Webmaster Tools. Verify the domain, submit /sitemap-index.xml, confirm all 18 pages are indexed within two weeks.
  • Test the enquiry form end to end from a phone on mobile data, not just desktop — and confirm the notification actually reaches a monitored inbox.
  • Set up the shared enquiry inbox so form mail does not land with one person who might be travelling.

Make it evidential

The site currently asserts an unbroken cold chain. This phase makes it visible. For a buyer comparing suppliers, one photograph of your own cold store with a thermometer in frame outperforms three paragraphs of copy.

  • Commission photography — the single highest-return item on this roadmap. One day with a photographer covers it: the BSCIC warehouse, cold store interiors, the grading table, loading and delivery, product close-ups for all ten categories, the Motijheel showroom, and proper portraits of the Proprietor and the CEO.

  • Build a Vendor Information Pack: one PDF containing the trade licence, all four certificates, company profile and bank details. Procurement teams ask for exactly this, and having it on a download link shortens onboarding by days.
  • Publish a general catalogue PDF — the full product list, no prices, updated quarterly. It is what a hotel purchasing manager forwards internally.
  • Google Business Profile for the Nikunja-2 head office and the Motijheel showroom, with photos and correct hours.
  • Add privacy-friendly analytics (Plausible or Umami — roughly 1 KB, no cookie banner needed under most regimes). Skip Google Analytics unless there is a specific reason: it is heavier, and it will not tell you anything more that matters here.

18 Conditional

Systems, only when triggered

Nothing here should be built on schedule. Each item has a trigger; build it when the trigger fires and not before, or the site accumulates machinery nobody uses.

  • Content management system. Trigger: someone other than the developer is asking for more than about two content edits a month. Then add Decap CMS — free, git-based, an admin panel at /admin, and it does not change how the site is built.
  • Bangla language layer. Trigger: analytics show meaningful Bangladeshi organic traffic bouncing off English pages. Until then it is unlikely to pay — the wholesale channel converts by phone and WhatsApp, and the corporate, hotel and export buyers all operate in English. Deferring is a real decision here, not an oversight.
  • Client price-list portal. Trigger: sales are spending real time emailing per-client price lists. Scope it as authenticated per-tier price lists, and expect it to need a server — this is where the static architecture stops being free.
  • CRM for enquiries. Trigger: more than roughly ten enquiries a month, or two people handling them. HubSpot's free tier or a shared Airtable base is enough; the form can post to both an inbox and an API.
04

Search direction

This business has two audiences searching in opposite directions, and they need different pages. Treating them as one audience is the most common way a trade site like this underperforms.

Audience A · Inbound

Bangladeshi buyers looking for a supplier

Purchasing managers at supershops, hotels, restaurants and institutional buyers. They search in English, on desktop, during office hours, and they are comparing two or three suppliers.

  • imported fruits supplier Dhaka
  • frozen salmon importer Bangladesh
  • cheese distributor Bangladesh
  • HORECA food supplier Dhaka

What wins them: the certificate pages, the facilities table, and photographs of the cold chain. They are checking whether you will pass procurement.

Audience B · Outbound

Overseas brands and buyers

Export managers at foreign food brands looking for a Bangladeshi distributor, and importers looking for Bangladeshi product. Higher value per enquiry, far less competition for the queries.

  • food distributor in Bangladesh
  • import agent Bangladesh food
  • Bangladesh mustard oil exporter
  • aromatic rice exporter Bangladesh

What wins them: the five-country office footprint, the represented-brands list, the export range, and a dedicated “sell your brand here” page.

The advantage worth protecting

Almost no Bangladeshi trading company publishes its certificate numbers, issue dates, surveillance schedule and full certified scope as crawlable HTML — they post a JPEG of the certificate, or nothing at all. The certifications page is therefore genuinely differentiated, and it is the page most likely to close a procurement evaluation. Keep it as text, keep the PDFs downloadable, and never move that content behind an image or a contact form.

The same applies to the sourcing origin index. It is generated from the product content, so it stays accurate for free — which means it can be trusted, which is the whole point.

What not to do

  • No AI-spun blog posts. This audience is a few hundred people. Publishing volume signals nothing to them and increasingly little to Google.
  • No duplicated city pages. “Food importer in Chittagong / Sylhet / Khulna” templates read as spam and risk the whole domain.
  • No prices. Competitors read websites too, and pricing varies by client tier and volume anyway.
  • No stock photography of foreign supermarkets. Buyers here can tell, and it undermines the one thing the site is selling: that this is a real operation with real infrastructure.
05

Technical direction

The guiding rule: stay static as long as the business allows. Every step away from static HTML adds a server, a bill, a security surface and a way for the site to be down.

When real photography arrives, switch to the image pipeline

Files in public/ are served as-is. Once actual photographs land, move them to src/assets/ and use Astro's <Image> component instead — it generates WebP and AVIF, builds responsive srcsets and keeps the layout stable automatically. Doing this at the point photos are added costs an hour; retrofitting it across fifty images later costs a day.

Performance budget

Hold each page under 250 KB total including images, and Largest Contentful Paint under 1.5 s on a 4G connection. That headroom exists today — the homepage is 12 KB gzipped — and photography is the only thing likely to spend it. A single unoptimised 3 MB JPEG undoes the entire architecture.

Dependencies

vite is pinned to ^6.4.1 deliberately. Vite 8 pulls in rolldown, whose native binary fails to install on some Linux setups and breaks the build outright. Astro 5 targets Vite 6, so the pin also keeps a single Vite in the tree and the type-checker clean. Do not bump it without running npm run build and npx astro check against a clean node_modules.

Keep the checks running

The build is verified by astro check (types) and an audit script that walks the built output for broken internal links, missing meta descriptions, duplicate <h1>s, images without alt text or dimensions, and invalid JSON-LD. Wire both into the deploy pipeline so a regression fails the build rather than reaching production quietly.

Backups and history

The project is not yet a git repository. Make it one before launch and tag a release at each content milestone. The certificates and the business profile PDFs should be committed alongside the code — they are the source of record for everything the site claims.

06

Growing with the group

The business profile commits to five expansions: a food production and processing plant, a hotel and restaurant chain, high-tech product representation, a supershop and retail chain, and automation engineering products. Only the first is adjacent to what the site currently describes. The others will break the current information architecture, and it is much cheaper to plan for that now than to rebuild later.

Expansion Fits current site? What it needs
Food production & processing plant Yes A manufacturing section, plus the plant added to the facilities table. The ISO and HACCP scope already covers manufacturing — the site simply does not lean on it yet.
Supershop / retail chain Partly Retail is a consumer audience, not a trade one. It needs its own brand, its own site, and a link from here — not a page bolted onto a B2B distributor.
Hotel & restaurant chain No Entirely separate audience and booking behaviour. Separate site, linked from a group page.
High-tech products No A food-safety-certified food distributor is the wrong frame for electronics. Separate division or site.
Automation engineering No Industrial B2B with a long sales cycle. Separate site.

The recommendation

Keep this site as World Club, the food trading company, and do not dilute it. When the second business line becomes real, add a small group landing page on a neutral domain that points to each company — the pattern the profile already implies with Brothers Trade Corporation as a sister concern.

That is the practical argument for choosing a neutral company domain in section 02: a group structure hanging off a domain named after one own-brand of one division will be awkward to explain for as long as it exists.

Brothers Trade Corporation is currently a single mention on the About page. If it is an active trading entity rather than a historical one, it deserves either a proper section here or its own page — worth deciding before launch, since it is a two-hour job now.

08

Operating cadence

A trade site does not need constant attention, but it does need scheduled attention. Without a rhythm, the failure mode is predictable: nothing changes for eighteen months and then everything is wrong at once.

Weekly Confirm every enquiry received has been answered within one business day — the site promises this explicitly on the contact page. Check the form still delivers by sending one test.
Monthly Review Search Console for coverage errors and the queries actually bringing people in. Refresh or extend one product category — new lines, corrected availability, a better photo.
Quarterly Update the headline statistics in company.json. Review the client list for additions and departures. Re-export the catalogue PDF. Run npm outdated and patch dependencies.
Annually DCCI membership renewal. Certificate surveillance audits and any re-issue. Full re-read of the About and Sourcing copy against how the business now actually works. Revisit this roadmap.
09

How to measure it

Pageviews are the wrong metric for this business. A hundred well-qualified visitors a month is a success; ten thousand unqualified ones is noise that will tempt bad decisions.

Track five things, monthly:

  • Qualified enquiries. Form submissions and WhatsApp conversations that name a real company and a real requirement. This is the number that matters.
  • Vendor-pack and certificate downloads. A strong buying signal — nobody downloads an ISO 22000 certificate casually.
  • Branded search impressions. Search Console impressions for “World Club” and variants, measuring whether the company is becoming known by name.
  • Enquiry source mix. Ask on every enquiry how they found you. It is the only reliable way to separate what the website did from what the sales team did.
  • Time to first response. Against the one-business-day promise the site makes.

A realistic target twelve months after launch, assuming phases 1 and 2 are delivered: eight to fifteen qualified enquiries a month, weighted toward Bangladeshi trade buyers, with one or two overseas brand-representation approaches a quarter. Those overseas approaches are worth disproportionately more than their volume suggests — a single new brand agreement can be worth more than a year of retail enquiries.
